The Search Evolution: from hand-built Boolean queries to asking a question in plain language

Search at Signal AI was so hard to build that our own teams did it for customers. Over five years, as the solo product designer, I moved it from keyword lists to AI tags that customers define themselves.

The problem

Search wasn’t built for users to own. We were asking them to become search architects.

7,560

hours Support & CS spent logged into customer accounts in 12 months, much of it building searches.

  • Hundreds of keywords per search. Users typed every variation of a term so that no mention was missed.
  • AI entities went unused. They were hard to discover and hard to trust, so users fell back on keywords.
  • Layers of linked searches. One business question became several searches only their author understood.

What I did

Five design shifts, each removing one thing users had to know.

01Year 2

From recall → to recognition

Problem
Hundreds of trained entities and topics existed, but users could only find them by their exact name.
What I designed
Ask in your own words, such as “more topics similar to Environment”, and Signal suggests the trained topics to add in one click.

02Year 3

From query syntax → to visual structure

Problem
A complex query was a wall of text. You could build it, but a teammate couldn’t read it back.
What I designed
The single query string became labelled rows: companies, topics and exclusions.

03Year 4

From system categories → to user-defined groups

Problem
Users could only group entities of the same type, so one question still needed several linked searches.
What I designed
Groups that mix companies, products and sources, each with its own AND, OR and NOT.

04Search 2.0

From constructing queries → to expressing intent

Problem
People don’t come to Signal to build searches. They come with questions about their business.
What I designed
Two steps: define a content universe, then describe what you’re looking for. An AI tag classifies every article.

05Latest

From hoping the AI understands → to validating that it does

Problem
Users couldn’t tell whether the AI had understood a definition before applying it to thousands of articles.
What I designed
Inside setup, users mark real borderline articles as tag or don’t tag. Their answers refine the definition.

Results

Advanced Searches is live in Beta, and cheap enough to offer every $100k+ client.

Client use case

Clearing adverts out of a client’s coverage

30,000 articles in 30 days, where 2 in 5 were adverts.

Before

  • Manual taggingToo much data, too slow, too costly, never “real time”.
  • Keyword searchAdverts change too fast for any Boolean to keep up.

Now

  • One promptFlags every advert across the client’s coverage.
  • Quick, clean exportAdverts can be filtered out straight from the CSV export.

Client use case

Tracking the narratives a client reports to its CEO

6 key narratives, reported to the CEO.

Before

  • Analyst trackingToo costly, and too slow for near real-time reporting.
  • Keyword matchingToo precise to catch the nuance of a message.

Now

  • Six AI tagsOne per narrative, refined in a few quick iterations.
  • Near real timeNarrative pull-through tracked on repeat.
